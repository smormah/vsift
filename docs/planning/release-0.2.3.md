# The patch release 0.2.3: the plan

Status: **the plan for the patch release `0.2.3` (cycle 2 of [`backlog-iterations.md`](backlog-iterations.md)) and the record of its evidence. Position on 2026-10-25: the first candidate, `0.2.3-rc.1`, was cut on 2026-10-22, tagged and published on 2026-10-24 (npm `next`), and its evidence is recorded below ("The evidence on 0.2.3-rc.1"): every hosted run passed, or failed and is explained, the maintainer decided the open items on 2026-10-25 (below), and the Node.js pins (#526) are accepted for `0.2.3` with L-232 (no `0.2.3-rc.2`). The stable `0.2.3` is not made.** It is the second candidate made in the weekly window of [`release.md`](../operations/release.md) 6.18 and the second made on a release branch: the maintainer's runbook for it is `release.md` 6.21. `0.2.2` stays npm `latest` until the stable is published; nothing is announced.

## What this release is, and why

`0.2.2` was published on 2026-10-18 (cycle 1). The maintainer's replan of 2026-10-21 ([ADR 0027](../decisions/0027-the-road-to-1-0.md), the first section of [`backlog-iterations.md`](backlog-iterations.md)) makes `0.2.3` **cycle 2, all of it**: the real-media test set, the follow-ups to the seams measured on real speech, the Windows private folders, the whisper.cpp re-pin and the dependency upkeep, cut at midnight on Friday 23 October (the maintainer's local time) and published about 26 to 27 October. **It is a patch release, not a work packet:** P00 to P14 stay complete, the claims rung stays `candidate`, and nothing here is a statement of support. **`0.3.0` work is not on `main`:** its pull requests stay drafts until the supervisor opens `main` after this cut.

## The window plan for 0.2.3-rc.1

The steps and their exact command lines are [`release.md`](../operations/release.md) 6.21. In order:

1. **The cut (midnight on Friday 23 October, the maintainer's local time):** the supervisor merges the cut's pull request to `main` before the night's nightly, waits for its `CI result` on the merge commit, dispatches the Nightly workflow on `main` while the merge commit is `main`'s head (or, if `main` has moved, on the new `release/0.2.x`) and waits for it to pass; then, **after the `Release window` check with `records=true`**, the maintainer deletes the old `release/0.2.x`, creates it at the merge commit and tags `v0.2.3-rc.1` there (annotated, `VSift 0.2.3-rc.1`).
2. **The dry run on the tag** (`guard` answers within a minute), then **the publish on the maintainer's approval** of the `release` environment. The publish moves `next` from `0.2.2-rc.1` to `0.2.3-rc.1` on all four packages; `latest` stays `0.2.2` and GitHub's latest release stays `v0.2.2`. The supervisor never approves the environment and runs no npm write.
3. **The quick checks of the published bytes,** right after the publish: `P14 verify release` (RQ-19), `P14 journeys` (RQ-05), `P13 managed smoke` (RQ-06) and `P14 published artifacts` from `0.2.2` and then from `0.2.2-rc.1` (RQ-01 to RQ-04), one after another.
4. **The long runs** (6.18 step 3). `crates/`, `Cargo.lock` and the workflows changed, so every item with them in scope needs a run on the tag. **This is the cycle that needs the monthly round** (a re-pin is a major change, [`backlog-iterations.md`](backlog-iterations.md) "How each kind of change is tested"): `P07 local ASR` (accuracy, the noisy-speech gate included), `P14 malicious media` (RQ-10), the dated `P14 scan reading` (RQ-13) and the full durability campaign (`P13 managed power loss` and `P10 durability campaign`, RQ-11), with `P14 compatibility`, `P14 local upgrade`, `Fuzz` (`seconds=3600`), `P14 stress`, `P14 load` and `P14 runbook walk`. All on the tag, staggered, hosted; **nothing runs on the maintainer's machine** (no load, no stress, no real-media transcription). `P14 real media` may be dispatched once on the tag as an advisory reading; it is not an evidence item. A failed run is a finding: an issue first, then a targeted fix on the release branch and a rerun of only that workflow (6.18 step 4).
5. **Agent-trial batches 2 and 3 are the maintainer's decision at the window.** The skill is unchanged, so the freeze of 2026-10-19 (`302930904439...`) is kept byte for byte and the cut moved the `0.2.2-rc.1` batches' records aside to `p14-agent-trials/batch-2-0.2.2-rc.1/` and `batch-3-0.2.2-rc.1/`. RQ-15 is `passed` for `0.2.2-rc.1` and stale for this candidate; RQ-16 is `planned` (L-179). Either run the batches on the published `0.2.3-rc.1` (`-Version 0.2.3-rc.1`, one at a time, on the maintainer's go and allowances, batch 3 with `-AllowGraderChange`; [`p14-batch-2-3-checklist.md`](p14-batch-2-3-checklist.md)) or decide a waiver for `0.2.3` (the maintainer's decision, recorded in the ledger, never the supervisor's).
6. **The records,** by a records pull request to `release/0.2.x` (the stable plan reads the ledger at the stable commit, 6.8), from what each run computed and never earlier; then the stable commit `0.2.3` on the branch (Monday or Tuesday is the target, 6.18 step 5), and after the publish the records change to `main`.

### RQ-05 on macOS: how the evidence was made (#520)

**What happened.** `P14 journeys` on the tag passed on Ubuntu and Windows and failed on macOS, twice (attempt 1, then a re-run of the macOS job alone): after nine checkpoints in normal time, the T-04 accuracy gates failed with a recognition deadline after the noisy-speech sets had met their gates, once after 2,626 s and once after 2,330 s (the whole job took 71 and 56 minutes). **Why.** The investigation compared the product with `0.2.2` and found no change: the recognition code, the whisper.cpp flags, the 120 s chunk deadline and the runner image are the same. What grew in cycle 2 is the test suite, which gained the noisy-speech set (#492), and the set no longer fits the slow hosted macOS runner. **The maintainer's decision of 2026-10-25: no `0.2.3-rc.2`.** After the test-driver fix (#523, which skips the set on hosted macOS) merged to `main`, `0.2.3` is qualified on macOS by `P14 journeys` run against the published `v0.2.3-rc.1` binary with the newer test driver (`tests_from=workflow-ref`, dispatched on `main` at the merge commit `2a683b0cba6a`). **What the evidence is and is not.** The binary under test is the published candidate (`vsift 0.2.3-rc.1 (09e40398894f)`, installed from the real registry on each system); only the test driver is newer than the tag. It passed on all three systems, macOS in 32 minutes with `p07_asr_gates` in 624 s and the skip logged for each model profile. The noisy-speech set is **not run on hosted macOS** (known limit L-230, on `main`): it is measured and gated on Ubuntu and Windows by `P07 local ASR`, which passed on the tag with both profiles and every gate met. The set has still never been shown on macOS. #520 was closed with the passing run; the setup-budget flake found on the way is #524 (open; it did not fail in any run above).

### RQ-13: the dated reading

The reading of 2026-10-25 ([`p14-scan-reading-2026-10-25.md`](p14-scan-reading-2026-10-25.md)) covers the cycle's dependency changes (`tokio`, `process-wrap`, `lzma-rust2`, `jsonschema`, the new `vsift-windows-security` crate and its three `unsafe` blocks, whisper.cpp v1.9.5) and the window since 2026-10-18. It names **no advisory for any of them at the locked version**: `cargo deny` is clean for both lockfiles; the only RustSec advisories that name a locked crate are two for `tokio` (RUSTSEC-2026-0384, which affects 1.54.0 only, and RUSTSEC-2026-0386, patched in 1.53.3, the locked version); GitHub's three alert stores are empty and CodeQL has 0 results at the tag's commit; no record names whisper.cpp v1.9.5, and CVE-2026-110810 (#399) is fixed by it. For FFmpeg, the ten records with a fix commit are cherry-picked into the shipped snapshot with identical patches; **CVE-2026-107678 (L-144, #351) now has an upstream fix merged on 2026-10-19 that the snapshot lacks**; one new FFmpeg-code record without a fix commit (CVE-2025-52290, an MPEG-TS input) is of the kind #272 and L-122 already accept. **One new finding, not in anything that ships: the Node.js versions pinned for the tests (22.23.3) and for the publish job (24.21.0) are older than the security releases 22.23.4 and 24.22.1 of 2026-10-20.** The workflows cannot change on the release branch (6.8), so the options were to accept it for `0.2.3` with a register entry and move the pins on `main` and in the hot fix `0.2.4`, or to cut `0.2.3-rc.2` with new pins. **The maintainer chose the first on 2026-10-25** ([#526](https://github.com/smormah/vsift/issues/526), [L-232](known-limits.md#l-232); see the decisions).

**What the runs show, and what they do not.**

- **They are hosted runs on a synthetic corpus.** Hosted images are not clean machines (L-112), the load run is not the 8-hour one, and every accuracy figure in these runs is from synthetic speech (L-022); no real recording is part of this window's evidence.
- **`P14 journeys` and `P07 local ASR`:** the T-04 gates hold on Ubuntu and Windows including the noisy-speech set. On macOS only the clean set is gated in the journeys (above). One figure moved from `0.2.2-rc.1`: base on Windows read a clean word error rate of 3.25% where it read 4.06% (one word fewer wrong); the gate is at most 10%. Why one word differs between two runs of the same model is not shown.
- **`Fuzz`:** 16 of the 31 targets found their last new coverage in the final tenth of their hour, so the hour is a floor, not a plateau (#17). A runner that loses communication is not a finding of its target.
- **`P14 stress` (#519):** one repetition in 1,500 on one system is a timing bound in a rejection test under load, not a hang and not a wrong answer; the assertion on the outcome passed. It is the third failure of this suite's family under load on Windows (#206, #312), and the maintainer decided on 2026-10-25 that it is a known issue of `0.2.3` (below); the item is recorded `waived` for `0.2.3`, not passed.
- **`P14 load`:** the mix differs from `0.2.2-rc.1`'s (24 rounds and 208 sessions where it was 23 and 291); no session was left initializing.
- **`P14 malicious media`:** the container's peak was 898 MiB, below the 1,024 MiB it was on `0.2.2-rc.1`; it counts the page cache of the 512 MiB copy and is not VSift's own use (16 MiB).
- **The fixes of the cycle are shown by tests and the runs above, not by field use:** the long Windows folders (#469), the private folders (#443) and the managed activation's retry (#439) are shown on one desktop and by hosted Windows runs, not by a rate; the managed upgrade to whisper.cpp v1.9.5 is shown on Ubuntu by one hosted run.

## What is weaker than it sounds

- **The coarse scopes:** `crates` is in the scope of most items, so "the long runs this week's changes touched" is nearly every long run; the window's cost is the cost of a full round for this candidate, and it is the monthly round besides.
- **The Windows fixes of the cycle (#469, #443, #439) are shown on one desktop and by tests;** hosted Windows is one run per workflow, not a rate.
- **The real-media evidence is a handful of recordings with hand-made labels** and, for the seams, one recogniser on one CPU model per hosted run ([L-200](known-limits.md#l-200)).
- **The release workflow's second use:** the `guard`, the re-check and the 20-minute read-back ran once against the real services (`0.2.2-rc.1` and `0.2.2`); this is the first window with an existing release branch to delete and make again.
- **L-133's window reopens for repository pages** that name the candidate before it is published (this page, the guide's first page, the changelog, the trial documents, the runbook and the register); the README, the installation guide, the launcher's README and the security policy are worded to be true both before and after the publish.
